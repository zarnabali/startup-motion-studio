# Image style — added automatically to every generated image (`tools/make_image.py`)

Edit the four code blocks for your brand; keep the headings (the tool finds blocks by heading).
Default = "Style lock" + "Avoid". With `--real` = "Real-photo lock" + "Real-photo avoid" — use it for anything that
must pass as a real photo (people's spaces, products in use, b-roll). AI-looking photos are the #1 reason owners
reject a film.

## 1. Style lock
```
Clean, premium product photography. Soft, directional natural light, gentle shadows, true-to-life colour.
Simple uncluttered composition with clear negative space for text. Real materials and textures, sharp focus on the
subject, realistic depth of field. Colour palette leaning on the brand neutrals with at most a small touch of the
accent colour.
```

## 2. Composition notes
- Leave clean space where on-screen text or UI will sit (say where in the spec: "left third empty").
- One subject per image. 16:9 for b-roll (1536×1024), 1:1 for cut-outs (1024×1024), 2:3 for cards.

## 3. Avoid
```
Avoid: any text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks or brand marks; people unless the spec allows them; plastic,
waxy or CGI look; over-saturated colour; HDR; heavy bloom or lens flare; teal-orange grade; symmetry that looks
staged; duplicated objects; warped hands or objects; clutter.
```

## 4. Real-photo lock
```
This must look like an UNEDITED SMARTPHONE PHOTO taken by a real person, not advertising, not a render.
Camera: phone main camera, handheld at chest height, auto exposure and white balance, deep phone depth of field,
mild sensor noise in the shadows, slight lens distortion near the edges, verticals 1-3 degrees off, casual imperfect
framing (something partly cut off at an edge). Light: whatever the room has - window daylight mixed with a warm lamp;
uneven, real shadows and falloff. Real life: lived-in details, real creases and wear, nothing perfectly aligned.
NO PEOPLE, hands or reflections of people - unless the spec explicitly allows them.
```

## 5. Real-photo avoid
```
Avoid: editorial or advertising look; symmetry; evenly spaced or colour-sorted objects; showroom perfection;
cinematic grade, teal-orange, HDR, glow, bloom, lens flare, dreamy bokeh, film-grain filter; plastic, waxy or CGI
textures; 3D-render look; over-sharpening; melted or impossible shapes; duplicated objects; any text, letters,
numbers, logos, labels, price tags or watermarks; neon; sparkles.
```
